What a review platform actually measures

A star rating aggregates what buyers could personally observe: whether the parcel arrived, how long it took, whether support answered, whether the item matched the listing. Those are real and they are worth knowing, because they are the parts of a transaction a stranger can confirm from their own experience.

What a rating cannot measure is what was in the container. No reviewer ran an assay. A five-star review and a one-star review can describe identical material and differ only on how fast it shipped, which means the rating and the product quality are two separate questions that the format quietly merges into one number.

Why ratings are easy to move and lab records are not

Review platforms are open to anyone with an email address, which makes a rating cheap to influence in both directions. A seller can solicit favourable reviews from satisfied buyers; a competitor can file unfavourable ones. Neither action requires anyone to be complicit beyond the person typing.

Compare the cost of moving a batch-matched testing record. A report hosted on a laboratory's own domain requires that laboratory to cooperate. A batch identifier printed on a vial has to agree with the document a stranger can pull up. A record that contains poor results has to be maintained against the seller's own commercial interest. Those are expensive; a review is free.

How to weigh the two kinds of evidence together

Use ratings for what they are good at. If several independent reports describe parcels that never arrived or support that never answered, that is a real signal about the operation and it should carry weight, because those reviewers observed it directly.

Use the testing record for the question ratings cannot reach. Pull the batch number from the packaging, find it on the laboratory's portal, and read the figure. If the two sources disagree in a way that matters to you, the one that required a third party to cooperate is the one that is harder to stage.

Does a missing review-platform profile mean a seller is hiding something?

Can I trust a rating that has very few reviews?

Treat it as a handful of anecdotes rather than a measurement. A small sample moves several points on one review, so the number is unstable in a way the displayed figure does not show you.
