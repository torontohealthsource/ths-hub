This is not a peptide, and the assay people expect does not apply

Enclomiphene is a small molecule supplied as a tablet, not a peptide supplied as a lyophilised powder. The peptide-purity method that produces the 99%-plus figures elsewhere in this catalogue is not the method used here.

Anyone searching this by name is deep enough to notice a page that pretends otherwise, which is most of them.

Three reports, and none carries a purity percentage

The published set holds three reports covering enclomiphene entries: batch ENC03 on a 12.5mg tablet entry reporting 11.11mg, and two Enclomiphene Citrate Tablets entries, batches 202512-01 and 202410-01, reporting 13.18mg and 12.51mg.

None states a purity figure. For a tablet the meaningful question is content — how much active is in the unit — and that is what these report.

Note the batch format, because it tells you something

Two of the three batches are dated identifiers, 202512-01 and 202410-01, rather than the short product codes used across the peptide lines. That is the format of a tablet manufacturing lot.

It matters practically: when you check your own unit, the identifier you are looking for will not look like the peptide batch codes, and a reader who expects an ENC-style code may conclude no report exists for their lot when one does.

What content figures let you conclude

A content result establishes that the measured active matches the labelled amount within the method's tolerance. It does not speak to related substances the way a peptide purity figure does, and it should not be quoted as if it did.

This is the same distinction that applies to the blends and to L-carnitine in this catalogue. The published record is not weaker on these items by accident — the products are different, so their documents answer different questions.
