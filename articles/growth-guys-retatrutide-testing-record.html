Why the catalogue-wide number is the wrong number

Published testing records are assembled batch by batch, and a set covering 244 reports across 133 distinct products is not uniform across those products. A headline average is a property of the set, not of any particular item in it.

So the useful question is never what a vendor averages. It is whether the specific batch you are holding has a document, and what that document actually measured. Those are answerable; the average is not an answer to either.

Finding the batch, not the product page

The batch or lot identifier is printed on the packaging. Take that identifier to the testing laboratory's own portal rather than to a page the seller controls, because a document hosted by the seller is a document the seller can edit.

In the Growth Guys set the reports resolve on Janoshik Analytical's portal, which is the structural property worth caring about: it requires a third party to cooperate. If a batch identifier does not resolve anywhere independent, treat that as the finding and act on it.

Reading what the report says, and what it is silent on

Read the assay headings first. A purity assay reports the proportion of stated compound and is usually run by chromatography; it is not a sterility result and not an endotoxin result, and treating a high purity figure as either is the most common misreading in this subject.

If an assay you care about is not present, the honest conclusion is that this batch was not tested for it. That is different from a result obtained and withheld, and different again from a failure. An absent assay is a gap you decide whether to carry.

What the weak results in the published set are worth

Of the 181 reports carrying a purity figure, the average is 99.03 percent, 33 fall below ninety-eight percent, and the lowest is 93.844. Those low numbers are the most load-bearing part of the record.

A published set containing nothing unflattering is consistent both with uniformly good material and with selective publication, and nothing inside the set separates them. A set that carries its own poor results is behaving like one published as the results arrive.

What if my batch number returns nothing on the portal?

That is a real finding. It means the independent record does not cover the item you are holding, which is the situation the batch identifier exists to rule out.

Does a purity result mean the material is sterile?

No. Purity and sterility are different assays. A report carrying one does not carry the other, and the headings are what tell you which was run.
