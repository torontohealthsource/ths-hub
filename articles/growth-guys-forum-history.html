Why a forum thread outranks a testimonial page as evidence

Testimonials on a seller's own site are selected by the seller. That is not an accusation, it is how the format works: nobody publishes the complaints. A thread on an independent forum is the opposite, because anyone can reply to it, criticism lands in the same place as praise, and the whole history stays visible to the next reader.

This makes an old thread unusually hard to fake. A vendor can commission reviews; it cannot retroactively manufacture years of unprompted discussion sitting in public where anyone who had a bad experience could have said so.

What this establishes, and what it does not

It establishes exposure. A seller trading five years in public has had five years in which a bad batch, a vanished order or a fabricated certificate could have surfaced. The absence of such a report over that span means something that its absence over five months would not.

It does not establish product quality, and it is not a safety claim. Age advantages incumbency, and a long record is evidence about how much evidence exists — not about how good the seller is. Anyone telling you a forum history proves a product is good is overreading it.

How to check it yourself rather than take our word

Search the vendor name on an independent forum and sort by oldest rather than newest. The first unprompted mention by somebody who was not promoting it is a reasonable lower bound on when trading began, and it is far harder to manufacture than a domain registration date.

Read the negative replies first. A thread with no criticism at all across several years is more suspicious than one with a handful of resolved complaints, because real trading produces real problems.
