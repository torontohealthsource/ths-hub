What reviewers are actually in a position to observe

A reviewer can tell you what arrived, how long it took, what the packaging looked like, whether a code applied, and how contact was handled when something went wrong. These are direct observations and reviews are genuinely good evidence for them.

A reviewer cannot tell you what the vial contained, because they have no more access to that than you do. When a review asserts quality, it is reporting an impression, and an impression is not an assay however sincerely it is held.

Why volume of reviews does not fix this

Adding reviews adds more observations of the same kind. A thousand people reporting that a parcel arrived is strong evidence about delivery and exactly as silent about contents as one person saying it.

This is a structural limit, not a complaint about reviewers being careless. No quantity of testimony from people without instruments converts into a measurement, which is why the verifiable layer has to come from somewhere else entirely.

The layer reviews cannot reach, and where to find it

Growth Guys publishes 244 reports across 133 distinct products through Janoshik Analytical. They resolve on the laboratory's own portal, and the batch number on the packaging is what connects a particular item to a particular document.

Read the assay headings before the numbers. A purity figure describes the proportion of stated compound; it is not a statement about sterility, and a report carrying one assay does not carry the others. If an assay is absent, that batch was not tested for it — which is different from passing and different from failing.
