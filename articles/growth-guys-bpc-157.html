The eleven without a figure are the blend entries, chiefly BPC-157 with TB-500. That is a method difference rather than a gap, and the next section is about why.

Why the blend versions carry no purity number

A blend contains two active compounds, so there is no single substance for a purity percentage to describe. The report states the recovered mass of the combination instead — for example batch BPCTB10, a 10mg/10mg blend, reporting 20.39mg.

Reading that report expecting one percentage and finding none is the commonest misreading of a blend COA. The document is not incomplete; it answers a different question, which is whether the stated quantity is present.

Match the batch, not the product name

Batch identifiers in this set include BPC08-10, BPC09 and BPC10, and the same batch can appear under more than one size — BPC09 is reported both as a 10mg entry at 12.23mg and as a 5mg entry at 5.84mg.

So the useful check is not whether a report exists for "BPC-157 10mg". It is whether a report exists for the identifier printed on the vial you were sent. A product name matches hundreds of vials; a batch matches yours.

Mass above label is the normal direction here

The reported masses sit above their labelled amounts across this group — 11.22mg and 12.53mg against 10mg labels, 12.23mg against another. Overfill is routine, for the same reason it is routine on every other line: underfilling generates complaints and overfilling does not.

What would be worth acting on is the reverse, a report coming in materially under label. Nothing in the twenty-two does.

The ten-second version

Open the report for your batch on the laboratory's own portal, confirm the identifier matches your vial, then read purity if it is a single compound and recovered mass if it is a blend. Do not compare a blend's mass figure against a single compound's purity figure; they are not the same measurement.
