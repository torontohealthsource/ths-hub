# Why a result in mice is a reason to run a trial, not a conclusion about people

Animal work is where a plausible mechanism is first demonstrated: that a substance reaches a tissue, binds what it was expected to bind, and produces a measurable change.

## They answer mechanism questions well

Animal work is where a plausible mechanism is first demonstrated: that a substance reaches a tissue, binds what it was expected to bind, and produces a measurable change. Those are real findings and they are the reason anything progresses to human study at all.

## Translation fails more often than it succeeds

Most interventions that work in animals do not work in people. This is not a scandal; it is the expected attrition of a pipeline whose early stages are deliberately permissive. The practical consequence is that an animal result raises the probability of a human effect from very low to merely low.

## Why the species matters

Metabolism, lifespan, immune function and the way disease is induced all differ. A condition created artificially in a short-lived animal may share a name with a human disease and little else, and the closer the model is to the real condition the more the result travels.

## Dose does not transfer directly

Converting a dose between species is not a matter of body weight. Standard conversions exist and are approximations, and a quantity described as equivalent is the output of a formula rather than an observation.

## Healthy young animals are the usual subjects

Laboratory animals are typically young, genetically uniform and otherwise well. Humans taking something are frequently none of those, and the difference changes both effect and risk in directions the study could not measure.

## Negative animal results carry weight

A substance failing in a well-designed animal model is informative, because the model was chosen to be favourable. Failure in easy conditions predicts failure in harder ones better than success predicts success.

## How coverage overstates it

Reporting routinely drops the species from the headline and restores it in the fourth paragraph. The finding is accurate; the impression is that something was shown about people, which is the one thing the study did not do.

## A reasonable way to weight one

Treat it as evidence that a hypothesis is worth testing. Ask whether any human trial followed, and if several years have passed without one, treat that absence as information too.
