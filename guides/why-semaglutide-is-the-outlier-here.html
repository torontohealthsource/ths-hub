An unusually deep published record

This compound has been through large, long, registered trials with tens of thousands of participants, published in major journals, followed by years of post-market observation across millions of people. In this index that is exceptional rather than typical.

Use it as the measuring stick

The practical value of listing it alongside the rest is contrast. When a summary says a compound is 'well researched', the question is whether it means this — registered phase three trials and a post-market record — or a dozen rodent papers. Both get described the same way.

Approval attaches to a product, not a molecule

What regulators approved is a specific manufacturer's formulation at defined doses. Material of the same molecule from a research supplier is outside that, and the published safety record does not transfer to it. This is the single most common error made about this compound.

What that makes the assay worth

More, not less. Where a licensed equivalent exists, the entire difference is provenance and composition — exactly what an independent report addresses.

The comparison worth drawing

Set it beside the trial-stage molecules in this index. Same class in some cases, same supply route, and an evidence base that is not remotely comparable. A purity figure says nothing about which side of that line a compound sits on.

A large regulated evidence base

Unlike most compounds in this category, this one has been through full regulatory assessment with published trial data and ongoing safety monitoring. That changes what can be said about it and what the appropriate sources are.

Why that cuts both ways

An extensive evidence base includes documented harms and contraindications that thinner literatures simply do not contain. More evidence means more known risks, and a shorter risk list elsewhere usually reflects less study rather than greater safety.
