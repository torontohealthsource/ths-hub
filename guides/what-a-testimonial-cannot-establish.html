A first-hand account is real evidence of something, and not of what it is used for

A testimonial is evidence that a person reported an experience. That is a genuine fact and a narrow one, and almost all the weight placed on testimonials rests on inferences well beyond it.

A testimonial is evidence that a person reported an experience. That is a genuine fact and a narrow one, and almost all the weight placed on testimonials rests on inferences well beyond it.

No comparison means no attribution

Without knowing what would have happened otherwise, improvement after something cannot be attributed to it. Conditions fluctuate, and people typically act when things are worst, which is also when they are most likely to improve regardless.

Selection happens twice

The person chose to write, and the publisher chose to display. Both filters favour the positive, and neither is visible in the resulting page however authentic each individual account is.

Detail is persuasive and not probative

Specific, vivid accounts feel more credible than general ones. Specificity correlates with memorability rather than with accuracy, and this is one of the better-documented findings about how people assess evidence.

Volume does not aggregate

A hundred selected accounts are a hundred instances of the same selection, not a sample of a hundred. Treating the count as strength mistakes the quantity of the filter's output for the quantity of evidence.

Where they are genuinely useful

For things a buyer can verify independently — whether delivery was slow, whether packaging arrived intact, whether support replied. Those are checkable claims and testimonials report them usefully.

Negative accounts carry more information

Where a publisher controls display, a visible negative account is a stronger signal than a positive one, because it survived a filter that had no reason to keep it.

How to use them

As a source of questions rather than answers. A recurring specific complaint across independent venues is worth investigating; a page of praise on a seller's own site establishes that the page exists.

Negative accounts carry more weight

Where a publisher controls what is displayed, a visible criticism survived a filter with no reason to keep it. That makes it a stronger signal than any number of favourable accounts on the same page.

Verifiable details are the usable part

Claims about delivery speed, packaging condition and support responsiveness can be checked against other sources. Claims about outcomes cannot, and the two are usually presented in the same paragraph.
