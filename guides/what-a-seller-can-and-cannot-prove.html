Some claims are checkable from outside

Whether a published document exists, whether a link resolves to a laboratory's own portal, whether a stated price appears at checkout, whether a batch number matches a certificate. These are verifiable by a reader without trusting the seller at all.

Some are checkable only over time

Consistency, whether figures published a year ago still match, whether failures were left visible. A reader can check these given a record, which is why a retained archive is worth more than any single page.

Some cannot be checked at all

Intent, care in handling, what happens to material between testing and dispatch. These are real and consequential, and no document a seller publishes can establish them, which is worth stating plainly rather than papering over.

Third-party documents shift the burden

A result resolving on a laboratory's own domain is harder to fabricate than a file hosted by the seller. The shift is not absolute and it is the single largest improvement available in this category.

Publishing failures is the strongest available signal

A record containing results that reflect badly is costly to produce and nearly impossible to fake convincingly, because the incentive runs the other way. It is the closest thing to a credible signal a seller can send.

Credentials are claims about process

An accreditation says a process was examined against a standard. It does not follow a specific batch, and conflating the two overstates what a credential covers.

Why the distinction matters to a reader

Effort spent checking unverifiable claims is wasted, and effort spent checking verifiable ones is cheap. Sorting claims into the three categories first makes the checking tractable.

What an honest seller does

States which category each of its claims falls into, and does not present the third as the first. That is itself a checkable behaviour.

Third-party resolution is the structural improvement

A document hosted on the laboratory's own domain, reachable without going through the seller, removes the seller from the chain at the point where tampering would be easiest. It is not absolute proof and it is the largest single improvement available in this category.

Consistency over time is the cheapest signal to check

Comparing what a seller published months ago with what it publishes now costs a reader nothing and cannot be faked retrospectively without leaving traces. A seller whose figures have moved without explanation has told you something no current page would.
