Age of the synthesis route matters

Molecules developed long ago have mature, widely published manufacturing routes. Purification is a solved problem, and more facilities can execute it correctly. Newer or structurally awkward compounds have neither advantage, which shows up directly in assay spread.

Clinically abandoned, which is a fact about development not quality

It was investigated and did not proceed to approval. Development programmes stop for commercial reasons as often as scientific ones, and 'did not reach market' should not be read as 'failed safety'. It does mean there is no late-stage trial literature.

High purity does not raise the evidence level

A very clean assay on a molecule with no phase three data leaves the outcome question exactly where it was. The two measurements are independent, and this compound is the clearest demonstration of it in the index.

Reading the number correctly

Expect high figures here and treat a low one as a genuine warning, because the synthesis difficulty does not explain it. The threshold for concern should differ per compound, which a single ranked column cannot express.

The published record for this item

Short sequences separate cleanly

Smaller, simpler molecules give sharper chromatographic peaks and fewer closely related impurities, so purity figures for them are both higher and more reliable. The number is easier to achieve and easier to trust.

Which is why cross-compound comparison misleads

Comparing purity across chemically different compounds rewards the easy syntheses. A sensible reading compares each against others of its own kind rather than against a flat bar.
