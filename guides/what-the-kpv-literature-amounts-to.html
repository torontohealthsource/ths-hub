Preclinical breadth is not depth

A compound studied many times in cells and animals can still have no human evidence at all. Counting papers measures attention, and the question that matters is how far up the evidence hierarchy any of them reach.

Why the gap persists

Moving from preclinical work to human trials requires funding and a commercial case. Absence of trials frequently reflects that economics rather than a scientific verdict, which is worth separating when reading a thin human literature.

Route of administration rarely transfers

Preclinical work often uses a route no person would use, and results from it do not carry across. Checking how a substance was administered in the cited studies frequently explains why a promising finding has not produced a human trial.
