Why mixed results are more informative than none

A compound with published disappointing trials is better characterised than one with no human data at all. Informal summaries tend to cite the encouraging studies and omit the rest, which inverts the usual relationship between how much is known and how confident a claim sounds.

Endpoint selection drives the disagreement

Where trials in an area disagree, the cause is often that they measured different things over different periods. Comparing the endpoints before comparing the conclusions resolves a large share of apparent contradictions in this literature.

Check for a registry entry

Trials registered before enrolment can be compared against what was eventually published, and a registered endpoint that disappears from the paper is a documented pattern worth looking for. An unregistered trial cannot be checked this way at all.
