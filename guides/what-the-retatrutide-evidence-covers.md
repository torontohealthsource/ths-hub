## Trial-stage, not approved anywhere

Retatrutide is an investigational molecule. It has not completed the approval process in Canada, the United States or the European Union, and the published human data comes almost entirely from trials run by the company developing it. That is normal for a molecule at this stage and it is a real limit on what can be said: there is no independent post-market safety record because there is no market.

## Company trial data is evidence, with a named bias

Sponsor-run trials are the standard route for a drug in development and are not thereby untrustworthy. The caution is narrower: the people designing the trial, choosing the comparator and writing it up have an interest in the result. Registration numbers and published protocols are what let a reader check whether the reported outcome matches the pre-stated one, and that check is available for this molecule.

## What a purity assay settles and what it leaves open

A high purity figure establishes that the material in the vial is overwhelmingly the stated compound. For a molecule still in trials, that is the question a buyer can actually get answered — whether the substance is what it claims to be. Everything about effect and safety at a given dose sits in a literature that is not yet complete.

## The distinction worth holding

Well-tested material and well-evidenced molecule are different claims. Our record speaks to the first. The second belongs to the trial registry, and at the time of writing it is still open.
