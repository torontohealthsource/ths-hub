## The literature is overwhelmingly preclinical

The published work on this peptide is dominated by rodent studies, many from a small number of research groups. Human trials are close to absent. This is the single most important fact about its evidence base and it is routinely skipped in summaries that cite 'over a hundred studies' without noting that nearly all of them are animal work.

## What an animal study can and cannot support

Rodent results establish a biological effect in rodents under the conditions tested. They are how almost every useful drug begins, and they are a reason to run human trials rather than a substitute for having run them. Dose, route and timescale rarely transfer directly, and the step that usually fails is exactly the one that has not been taken here.

## Repetition is not replication

A finding reproduced by the same group with the same model is weaker evidence than the same finding reproduced independently. When counting studies on this compound it is worth counting research groups instead, which produces a much smaller number.

## Why the purity number is still worth having

Knowing the material is what it claims to be is a precondition for any question about effect. It does not advance the effect question at all. A reader who takes a high purity figure as reassurance about outcomes has substituted the measurement that was available for the one they wanted.

## What would change the picture

A registered, adequately powered human trial with published results. Until one exists, the honest description is a compound with substantial animal evidence, minimal human evidence and wide informal use — three facts that are usually collapsed into one claim.

## A large preclinical literature, little else

Counting publications gives an impression of a well-studied compound. Sorting them by study type usually shows the weight sitting at the bottom of the hierarchy, which is the more useful summary.

## Concentrated authorship

Where a literature comes largely from a small number of connected research groups, independent replication is weaker than the paper count suggests. Checking author overlap across the main citations takes a few minutes and often changes the picture.
