## Approval is about a specific product, not a molecule in general

What is approved is a particular manufacturer's product, at stated doses, for stated indications, with a known formulation and supply chain. Material of the same molecule from another source is not covered by that approval, and treating the two as equivalent is the most common error made about compounds in this category.

## Why that makes the purity question more important here, not less

Where a well-characterised approved alternative exists, the gap between it and research-supply material is entirely one of provenance and composition. That is precisely what an independent assay addresses, which makes the published report the most informative single document available for this item.

## Reading the trial literature honestly

The published outcomes come from supervised trials with monitoring, defined titration and selected participants. Effect sizes from that setting are not a prediction for unsupervised use, and the gap is usually larger than summaries imply.
